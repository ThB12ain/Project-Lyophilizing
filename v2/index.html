Why lyophilize?

Take the water out, and most of the problems leave with it.

Most reagents and biologics are mostly water. Water lets molecules move, react and break down, which is why liquid products need freezers, cold packs and short expiry dates.

Lyophilization freezes the product, then lowers the pressure so the ice turns straight into vapor without melting. What remains is a dry, porous solid that holds its activity for months or years and comes back to life with a little water.

Before
Liquid, often 80–99% water
After
Dry solid, typically < 2% moisture
Storage
Often ambient instead of −20 °C

Benefits

What a dry product does for you.

The gains show up across the supply chain, from the warehouse to the bench of the person using your product.

Drop the cold chain

Room-temperature products ship by standard courier with no dry ice, freezers or temperature excursions to investigate.

Lose less product

Fewer thaw failures and fewer expired lots mean more of every batch reaches a customer.

Longer shelf life

Removing water slows degradation, often turning months of shelf life into years.

Simpler to use

Pre-measured, ready-to-reconstitute formats remove pipetting steps and the errors that come with them.

Lighter to ship

A dry kit ships without the weight of liquid, ice packs and insulated boxes, which cuts freight cost and carbon.

Exact doses

Each bead, cake or well holds a fixed amount of reagent, so every test starts with the same dose.

Is it a fit?

Is lyophilization right for your product?

Not every product should be freeze-dried. We would rather tell you early than spend your budget finding out.

Usually a good fit

  • Loses activity in liquid or after freeze-thaw
  • Ships frozen or on cold packs today
  • Needs a long shelf life or global distribution
  • Is used in small, exact doses

Needs work first

  • Stored in glycerol, which does not dry and must be removed
  • High in salt, which lowers the collapse temperature
  • Contains volatile or oily components
  • Already stable for years at room temperature

FAQ

Questions about lyophilization.

What is lyophilization?

It is freeze-drying. The product is frozen, then held under vacuum so the ice turns directly into vapor. A gentle final warming removes the remaining bound water. The result is a dry solid that keeps its structure and activity.

How much does a lyophilization project cost?

It depends on your product, format and volume. Feasibility is a small, fixed-scope study, and we quote development and production once we know how your product behaves. Send us a short description and we will give you a range.

How long until we know if our product can be freeze-dried?

A typical feasibility study takes four to six weeks from receiving material to a written recommendation.

Can you freeze-dry a product that contains glycerol?

Glycerol does not dry, so it has to be removed or replaced before lyophilization. We do this as part of feasibility, usually by buffer exchange and adding a sugar such as trehalose.

Our product failed freeze-drying elsewhere. Can you help?

Often, yes. Failures usually trace back to the formulation or a cycle that ran above the collapse temperature. We start by measuring those temperatures and reviewing what was tried before.

Do you work under cGMP?

Yes. Production runs under our quality system, and development work is documented so it can support your regulatory filings.

Can you support CE-IVD or FDA submissions for diagnostics?

We provide the manufacturing, validation and stability documentation your regulatory team needs. Your company remains the legal manufacturer of the finished product.

Do you also do analytical testing?

Yes. We test residual moisture, appearance, reconstitution, functional performance and stability in house.

Will you take over a cycle developed at another site?

Yes. We review your recipe and data, run engineering batches on our equipment and adjust for differences before production.

Do you sell your own products?

No. We only manufacture for our clients, so we never compete with you.
