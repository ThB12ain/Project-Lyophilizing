  • Positive and negative external controls

FAQ

Questions about lyophilization.

What is lyophilization?

Lyophilization removes water from a frozen product by sublimation under vacuum, so ice turns directly into vapor without melting. A final secondary drying step removes bound water. The result is a dry, porous solid that keeps its structure and activity and rehydrates quickly.

What are lyophilized beads?

They are small spheres of lyophilized reagent, each holding one exact dose. A bead drops into a tube, well or cartridge and dissolves when the sample or buffer is added.

How does lyophilization help point-of-care diagnostics?

Reagents stay stable at room temperature, the user skips pipetting and mixing steps, and each test starts with the same dose. That makes tests easier to ship, store and run outside a lab.

What are lyophilized control swabs?

They are swabs coated with a known organism or target and then lyophilized. They act as positive or negative controls that behave like a real patient swab and store at room temperature.

Can you lyophilize a reagent that contains glycerol?

Glycerol does not dry, so it has to be removed or replaced first. We do this during feasibility, usually by buffer exchange and adding a lyoprotectant such as trehalose.

Our reagent failed lyophilization elsewhere. Can you help?

Often, yes. Failures usually trace back to the formulation or to a cycle that ran above the collapse temperature. We start by measuring those temperatures and reviewing what was tried before.

Do you sell your own products?

No. We only manufacture for our clients, so we never compete with you.
